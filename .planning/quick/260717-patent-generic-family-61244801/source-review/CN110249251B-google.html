The multi-element imaging lens may be formed from five plastic elements and an optional empty or relatively low refractive power sixth plastic element. The lens can use selected plastic materials to reduce thermal focus shift. In a lens, the negative refractive power elements may be formed of plastic materials with relatively large negative refractive index changes with temperature (referred to as dn/dT for short), while the positive refractive power elements may be formed of plastic materials with relatively small negative dn/dT material formation. As disclosed, reducing thermal focus shift can eliminate the need for an autofocus device such as a voice coil. As disclosed, reducing thermal focal shift can also eliminate the need to use one or more glass lenses to further reduce thermal focal shift, which can reduce costs for the lenses.

Technical Field
The present disclosure relates to imaging lens assemblies.
Background
It is difficult to design and manufacture an imaging lens assembly that maintains focus over a wide temperature range for which it is suitable. For example, at a particular temperature, a thermal change in the refractive index of the lens element may translate the focal plane of the imaging lens element towards or away from the sensor, which results in a blurred image on the sensor.

Detailed Description
The multi-element imaging lens may be formed from five plastic elements and optionally a hollow or relatively low refractive power sixth plastic element. The lens may use a plastic material selected to reduce thermal focus shift. In the lens, the negative refractive power element may be formed of a plastic material having a negative refractive index variation (abbreviated as dn/dT) that is relatively large (absolute magnitude) with temperature, and the positive refractive power element may be formed of a plastic material having a negative dn/dT that is relatively small (absolute magnitude). As disclosed, reducing thermal focus shift may eliminate the need for an auto-focus device (such as a voice coil). As disclosed, reducing the thermal focus shift may also eliminate the need to use one or more glass lenses to further reduce the thermal focus shift, which may reduce the cost for the lens.
In the following discussion, the Abbe number (also referred to as the V number) of a material is defined as a dimensionless quantity (n)d-1)/(nF-nC) Where nd is the refractive index of the material at a wavelength of 587.6nm (helium d-line), nFIs the refractive index of the material at a wavelength of 486.1nm (hydrogen F line), and nCIs the refractive index of the material at a wavelength of 656.3nm (hydrogen C-line). Generally, the higher the Abbe number of a material, the lower the dispersion of the material.
In the following discussion, the quantity dn/dT is used to represent the change in the refractive index (n) of a material with respect to temperature (T). The amount dn/dT is evaluated at a temperature of 25C at a wavelength of 587.6nm (helium d-line). The higher the value of dn/dT, the greater the change in refractive index with temperature. In the discussion that follows, when reference is made to the magnitude of the amount dn/dT, it will be understood that this magnitude corresponds to the magnitude of the absolute magnitude of the amount dn/dT. The relatively large value of dn/dT is further away from zero than the relatively small value of dn/dT. In addition, it will be understood that the values of Abbe number and dn/dT may vary slightly from batch to batch of optical material. While optical material manufacturers typically attempt to minimize lot-to-lot variations, some variations are practically unavoidable. The values of Abbe number and dn/dT discussed herein are public values specified by the material manufacturer.
The following discussion refers to various optical plastic materials, all of which are commercially available and all of which have well-defined and well-recorded optical and mechanical properties readily known to those of ordinary skill in the art. The plastic materials discussed herein may be formed from one or more polymeric materials and/or one or more plastic materials. In some examples, the plastic materials discussed herein do not include glass materials and/or do not include fused silica.
